# QEVIK — 0→100 ROADMAP + CASE STUDIES

## 1. 0→100 model

Qevik should answer:

> What should this business do next to move from its current state toward its goal?

This is evidence-driven, not a generic checklist.

Inputs:
research, evidence, business model, opportunities, capabilities, goals, dependencies, customer tasks, Qevik tasks, measurement.

## 2. Roadmap dimensions

Website, UX, technical health, SEO, AI visibility, entity presence, content, image, video, social, ecommerce, marketplaces, advertising, leads, CRM, email, analytics, conversion, automation.

Unmeasured ≠ bad. Low confidence must not become a low score.

## 3. Task types

### CUSTOMER_TASK
Examples:
- connect payment gateway;
- create/verify social account;
- grant analytics/ad access;
- provide brand assets;
- approve publication.

### QEVIK_TASK
Examples:
- technical SEO;
- schema;
- content;
- AI visibility optimization;
- image generation;
- ad preparation;
- lead capture;
- measurement.

Each task should have owner, status, dependency, evidence, expected outcome and measurement.

## 4. 10-day roadmap

Use for activation:
- baseline/access;
- highest-impact fixes;
- conversion/lead capture;
- content/media foundations;
- measurement;
- review and next plan.

Actual tasks must be evidence-derived.

## 5. 30-day roadmap

Example:
Week 1 foundation;
Week 2 visibility/conversion;
Week 3 content/media/leads;
Week 4 campaigns/measurement/optimization.

Template only; recommendations must be research-derived.

## 6. 90-day roadmap

Foundation → Visibility → Acquisition → Conversion → Retention → Measurement → Re-evaluation.

## 7. Website example

After building a website, possible customer tasks:
- payment gateway;
- social account;
- analytics;
- business verification;
- brand assets.

Possible Qevik tasks:
- technical SEO;
- schema;
- content;
- AI visibility;
- images;
- lead capture;
- analytics;
- ad preparation.

Exact sequence must come from evidence.

# 8. AHS

Premium catering / established company.

Expected behavior:
- strong business;
- strong brand;
- FAST site;
- deep content/media;
- limited website opportunities where evidence supports them.

Known research-backed opportunities include:
- buried portfolio;
- contact friction;
- missing hreflang;
- blog/content weaknesses;
- discoverability improvements.

Do not manufacture ecommerce or generic weaknesses.

# 9. Coffee shop

Potential:
local search, Google Business, menu, directions, reviews, local content, social, ordering/conversion.

Do not assume ecommerce.

# 10. Ecommerce seller

Potential:
product opportunity, listing quality, imagery, video, SEO, marketplace presence, ads, measurement.

Lifecycle:
Research → opportunity → listing → images → video → SEO → QA → marketplace → ads → measurement.

# 11. B2B company

Potential:
service pages, case studies, B2B SEO, AI visibility, lead capture, enrichment, CRM integration, email.

# 12. Logistics company

Potential:
service/location pages, quote journey, lead generation, B2B targeting, CRM integration, analytics.

# 13. Lead/CRM business

Potential:
lead acquisition, qualification, enrichment, pipeline, automation, email, measurement.

Prefer integration over rebuilding a complete CRM.

# 14. Small service business

Potential:
website, local visibility, service pages, lead form, reviews, content, basic ads, analytics.

# 15. Demo requirement

Different businesses must receive materially different recommendations.

Never produce the same generic package for everyone.
