## What was built

`website/imagery.py` and `execution/capabilities/imagery.py`.

The hard problem is not generation. **An image on a business's own website is a
claim about that business.** A kitchen photograph on a caterer's site says *this
is our kitchen*. Put a generated one there and the site makes a false statement
in the customer's name, to their customers — and unlike invented copy, nobody
reads an image sceptically. Catching it requires knowing what the building looks
like, which the reviewer usually does not.

| | Slots | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| **Documentary** | premises, team, product, work, equipment, certificate, food | Only a photograph the business supplied. Nothing generated, whatever the prompt. |
| **Decorative** | header_texture, section_pattern, divider, background | Generated is honest — it asserts nothing — and is labelled anyway. |

The distinction is structural: `SLOTS` is a registry, `documentary()` is a
property of the slot, and `ImageRequest` carries no flag that could override it —
asserted by a test, so nobody can mark a premises photograph decorative. An
unknown slot is documentary, because guessing wrong publishes a false claim
rather than losing a decoration.

Provenance reaches the page: every `<img>` carries
`data-provenance="generated:provider/model"` or `"supplied"`. The question "is
this photograph real" gets asked while looking at the page.

The executor produces a **plan** rather than spending money — what can be made,
what the customer must photograph, what it would cost, with UNKNOWN where a
provider reported nothing. Documentary images are never placed automatically:
where a premises photograph belongs is a decision about the business.

`offer-imagery` joins `REQUIRES_CUSTOMER_INPUT`. **All six offers now have
executors.**
